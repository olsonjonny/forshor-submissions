\section*{Objective}
PhaseTripleProduct board: minimise $Q \times T$ for one phase triple product on three $n = 2048$-bit registers, $\delta = 0.01$, at the board's exact widths.

\section*{Starting point}
The starting policy (one band, $k = 4$, the planner's optimum over the tables in the Parallel sequences appendix of Kahanamoku-Meyer and Yao (2024)) was the baseline we compared against.
Our two tables are new: neither reuses a table or a program from that paper, though they use the same kind of evaluation points.

\section*{Where the cost is}
With the digital triple-product leaf, a leaf of width $w$ costs about $12w^2$ Toffolis and $3w-2$ rotations, and the leaves carry about 97\% of the Toffolis of the policy below.
A level of arity $k$ replaces one product of width $w$ by $3k-2$ products of width about $w/k + g$, where the guard $g$ is set by the largest evaluation point.
So $T$ is driven by $N \cdot w_{\mathrm{leaf}}^2$ (number of leaves times leaf width squared), while $Q = 3n + 3\sum_{\text{levels}} (k\, w_{\mathrm{child}} - w) + 3$ grows with each level's guard.

\section*{Construction}
Two bands: $k = 6$ for $w \ge 1024$ and $k = 4$ for $128 \le w < 1024$; the leaf below.
At $n = 2048$ the exact ladder is $2048{:}6 \to 359{:}4 \to 97{:}\mathrm{leaf}$, so there are $16 \times 10 = 160$ leaves of width 97 (the starting policy has 100 leaves of width 138).

\emph{Points.} The contract admits $0$, $\infty$, $\pm 2^e$ and $\pm 2^{-e}$. For each table we took the $3k-2$ admissible points of smallest exact demand at the width where the table is used
(the top limb is signed and its width is $w - (k-1)\lfloor w/k \rfloor$, so integer and reciprocal points of the same size differ in demand):
\begin{itemize}
  \item $k = 6$ (at 2048): $0, \infty, \pm 1, \pm 2, \pm 4, \pm 8, \pm\tfrac12, \pm\tfrac14, \pm\tfrac18$;
  \item $k = 4$ (at 359): $0, \infty, \pm 1, \pm 2, \pm\tfrac12, \pm\tfrac14$.
\end{itemize}

\emph{Programs.} Integer points are accumulated in register $0$ (row $p^j$), reciprocal points in register $k-1$ (row $m^{k-1-j}$), each by \texttt{addScaled} with a shift, and undone at the end.
Within each register the points are visited in the order that minimises the number of additions between consecutive rows (signed-digit weight of the row differences, a shortest tour from and back to the identity row).
There are no \texttt{negate} operations, which this cost model charges about $12w$ Toffolis each against about $w$ for an addition: 86 additions for $k = 6$ and 30 for $k = 4$.
No intermediate row exceeds the demand of the points, so each table's exact child width equals the width its points force (359 at 2048 and 97 at 359).

\section*{Prices}
\texttt{starter.py price} (via \texttt{forshor policy ptp}) printed for this policy:
\begin{itemize}
  \item board (exact widths): $T = 77{,}377{,}634$; ladder $2048{:}6 \to 359{:}4 \to 97{:}\mathrm{leaf}$;
  rotations $46{,}240 \times 72.0177$ T, Toffoli-class $18{,}511{,}884 \times 4$ T; engine 2 $77{,}377{,}634$ T (agree);
  \item $Q = 6{,}552$ (live-qubit peak, formula); score $Q \times T = 5.069783 \times 10^{11}$;
  \item cross-check (scanned widths, not the score): $485{,}355{,}383$ T, ladder $2048{:}6 \to 456{:}4 \to 152{:}4 \to 76{:}\mathrm{leaf}$.
  The scan charges a bit per addition along a chain, so it is pessimistic for these long addition chains.
\end{itemize}
The starting policy prices at $T = 97{,}630{,}311$, $Q = 6{,}339$, $Q \times T = 6.188785 \times 10^{11}$ (ladder $2048{:}4 \to 520{:}4 \to 138{:}\mathrm{leaf}$).

\section*{What did not help}
Priced in-process with the pack's own engine (\texttt{tcost.score.score\_policy} and \texttt{frontier.live\_peak\_qubits}, the functions \texttt{starter.py price} calls), over every sequence of 1 to 4 levels with $k$ from 2 to 8:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a wider first level ($k = 7$ or $8$) lowers $T$ but its guard (29 or 36 bits) costs more in $Q$; for example $2048{:}8 \to 292{:}4 \to 80$ gives $T \approx 7.36 \times 10^7$ but $Q = 7{,}095$;
  \item a third level is worse: leaves near 97 bits are already close to the balance between $12w^2$ Toffolis and the rotations and additions of a further split;
  \item $k = 5$ needs 13 points, one more than the exponents $|e| \le 2$ provide, so its guard jumps by about 4 bits;
  \item widening the first level's output with a T-free shift pair (so the next split is more even) did not lower any leaf width here.
\end{itemize}

\section*{What is not proved}
T counted in Python (Qualtran), not proved in Lean.
Q is the live-qubit peak, a formula over the policy's widths, not proved in Lean for this policy.
The site prices the policy again; its numbers are the ones that count.
