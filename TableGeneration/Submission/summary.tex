\section*{Objective}
A PhaseProduct policy aimed at the full order-finding board ($Q \times T$ of the single run, $\delta = 0.01$); it also enters the PhaseProduct board.

\section*{Where the cost is}
The full run is not one ladder from 2348. Its T is spread over 64 product widths: the balanced $2348 \times 2348$ products of the modular multiplications (about a quarter of T) and, mostly, the QFTs, which the IR builds from phase products of widths 2200, 1101, 551, 276, 139 and smaller (the QFT on the 4398-bit scratch register alone is about half of T).
So a policy is a choice of table at every width, and a single top-down ladder is the wrong object to optimise.

\section*{Construction}
\emph{Tables.} One table per $k$ for $k = 2, \dots, 7$, points only $0$, $\infty$, $\pm 2^e$, $\pm 2^{-e}$, chosen by least exact demand at a tuning width ($k = 3$ at 1101, $k = 7$ at 70, the others at 2048).
Programs for $k \ge 4$ keep the even part of the evaluation in the base register and the odd part (divided by $p$) in a helper register, so $+p$ and $-p$ cost one shifted addition each; programs for $k \le 3$ visit their points in the order with the fewest additions. No \texttt{negate}.
Additions: 2, 8, 20, 35, 54, 77 for $k = 2, \dots, 7$.

\emph{Bands.} A dynamic programme over every width reachable from the run's 64 product widths chooses, at each width, the leaf or the $k$ that minimises $\sum_{\text{sites}} \mathrm{count} \times (4\,\text{Toffoli} + 122.5\,\text{rotations})$, with node and leaf counts taken from the pack's engine; the choices become 20 bands:
$2348{:}6$, $2049{:}7$, $1175{:}5$, $1101{:}6$, $1025{:}7$, $588{:}4$, $514{:}5$, $513{:}4$, $404{:}5$, $316{:}4$, $295{:}3$, $148{:}4$, $139{:}3$, $129{:}4$, $120{:}3$, $112{:}4$, $75{:}3$, $70{:}2$, $66{:}3$, $65{:}2$ (threshold:$k$), leaf below 65.
The qubit peak comes from the $2049 \times 2348$ products of Steps 1, 2, 4 and 5 and their chain $2348{:}6 \to 404{:}5 \to 90{:}3 \to 33$; restricting that chain to fewer qubits ($k = 5$ or $4$ at the top, or $k = 4$ at 404) cost more in T than it saved in $Q$.

Our previous PhaseProduct entry, olsonjonny\_sub4 (bands $400{:}5$, $64{:}3$), priced at $2.144894 \times 10^{16}$ on the full run.

\section*{What did not help}
Tuning each table's points at any of the run's product widths (one width per $k$, coordinate descent priced on the full run) gave no gain beyond the tuning above; a $k = 7$ or $k = 6$ first split with other second levels, and capping the top $k$, were worse.
The even/odd programs were the last step: they removed about 5\% of the table additions, which are about 3\% of the run's T.

\section*{What is not proved}
T counted in Python (Qualtran), not proved in Lean.
Q is the live-qubit peak, a formula over the policy's widths, not proved in Lean for this policy.
The full-run census comes from ForShor's IR, matched to the compiled circuit at sampled widths, not proved at $n = 2048$.
The site prices the policy again; its numbers are the ones that count.
